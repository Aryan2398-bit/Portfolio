Overview

At [Company], we offer lending products to over 5 Cr+ users across India. Our loan application onboarding flow had accumulated years of incremental changes — each reasonable in isolation, but collectively creating a fragmented, high-friction experience that was silently killing conversions.

We set out to redesign the onboarding flow end-to-end, not just patch it — starting from user research and ending with a shipped product that measurably improved completion rates.

Problem

Increase onboarding completion with less tech/product effort.

Drop-off was happening across 9 distinct points in the flow. The business had been treating each drop-off as an isolated bug. We needed to understand them as a system.

Research

Funnel data

With our funnel data we reached to conclusion that:

Insights from funnel

Reinvest in the same flows

Users who completed the first step had significantly higher completion rates — the top-of-funnel experience was driving most drop-off.

Document upload was the biggest blocker

Over 40% of drop-offs happened at the KYC document step — users either didn't have documents ready or the upload flow was too complex.

Returning users convert more

Users who had partially completed and returned converted at 2.3x the rate of first-time visitors — suggesting the flow needed better save & resume.

Mobile drop-off higher than desktop

Mobile completion was 34% lower than desktop, pointing to form factor issues specific to the mobile flow design.

Research

Know from the users

Customers were divided into two segments:

  • NMF — New to lending: Users who had never taken a loan before and were unfamiliar with the process and documentation required.
  • EMF — Existing loan users: Users who had taken loans before, either with us or another lender, and had a clear mental model of what to expect.
Insights from session recordings

Lack of action

Users hovered over form fields but hesitated to fill, indicating information overload or lack of clarity about what was being asked.

Inefficient progress signals

Users interacted repeatedly with the progress bar but couldn't determine how many steps were remaining, causing anxiety and abandonment.

Rapid scrolling

Rapid scrolling patterns were observed before document upload steps, indicating users were looking for guidance that wasn't there.

Back-navigation confusion

Users frequently tapped back expecting to edit previous inputs, but were taken to the start of the flow instead — causing rage quits.

Insights from customer interviews

Trust anxiety

First-time borrowers were uncomfortable sharing Aadhaar and PAN without understanding why each document was needed.

Lack of awareness about eligibility

Users didn't know if they'd qualify before going through the full flow — they wanted an eligibility check upfront before submitting documents.

Document unpreparedness

Users often started the flow without their documents at hand, hit a wall, and abandoned. They needed a "get ready" state before the actual flow.

Insights from current flow review

Redundancy and inconsistent data

The same information was being asked multiple times across different steps — users had to enter their phone number three times in a single flow.

Overwhelming first fold with low-value content

The first screen showed compliance disclaimers and secondary information that added no value to a user trying to start their application.

No eligibility indicator

Users completed 6+ steps only to find out at the end they weren't eligible — there was no soft eligibility check anywhere in the flow.

Research

UX prioritization — we can't do it all

As a startup, we're all about moving fast — building quicker, breaking barriers even faster, and shipping like we should've done it yesterday 🚀. With limited engineering bandwidth and a 12-week timeline, we had to be ruthless about what we'd tackle first.

We used a prioritization framework — mapping ideas against effort vs. impact — to decide what would go into this sprint vs. the backlog.

Define

Synthesising research into opportunities

After mapping all our research findings, we reframed the problems as "How Might We" questions — turning friction points into design opportunities the team could rally around.

...make users feel confident about sharing sensitive documents before they start filling the form?
...let users know their eligibility before they invest time in a 10-step flow?
...reduce cognitive load on each screen so users don't feel overwhelmed mid-flow?
...let users save progress and return without losing their data or starting over?
...make the number of remaining steps feel manageable, not intimidating?
...redesign document upload so users know exactly what to prepare before they begin?
Define

Design principles we committed to

Before any wireframe was drawn, we aligned the team on three non-negotiable principles that would guide every decision in the redesign.

01
Trust before ask
Every screen that asks for sensitive data must first explain why it's needed, what it's used for, and how it's protected.
02
Show the end from the start
Users should always know how many steps remain, what they'll need to complete, and what happens after they're done.
03
One thing at a time
Each screen asks for exactly one piece of information. No multi-field forms. No scrolling required to find the CTA.
Ideation

Redesigning the flow from scratch

We scrapped the existing flow entirely and rebuilt from first principles. The new flow introduces a soft eligibility check upfront, a document checklist before data entry begins, and a persistent progress indicator throughout.

01
New

Soft eligibility check

A 3-question pre-qualification screen (age, income, employment type) before any personal data is collected. Users know in 30 seconds if they're likely eligible.

02
New

Document checklist screen

A dedicated "Get ready" screen listing every document needed, before any form fields appear. Users can bookmark and return when prepared.

03
Redesigned

One question per screen

Every data-entry step is a single field with a visible label, clear input hint, and an explanation of why it's needed — especially for PAN and Aadhaar.

04
Redesigned

Persistent numbered progress bar

A step indicator showing "Step 4 of 8" replaces the ambiguous progress bar. Users can tap completed steps to edit without losing downstream data.

05
New

Save & resume state

Progress is saved after each step. Returning users land on a "Welcome back" screen that picks up exactly where they left off with a summary of what's done.

06
Redesigned

Review & submit screen

A final consolidated summary before submission — all entered data visible at once, with inline edit links. No surprises at the end.

Design

Before vs. after

The most significant changes weren't visual — they were structural. Here's how the key problem areas changed.

Before
No eligibility signal — users completed 6+ steps before learning they didn't qualify.
Document surprise — PAN and Aadhaar asked mid-flow with no prior warning.
Ambiguous progress — a fill bar with no step count, no completion estimate.
No save state — leaving the app at any point meant starting from scratch.
Redundant data entry — phone number asked 3 times across 3 different screens.
After
30-second soft check — eligibility confirmed before any personal data is shared.
Document checklist upfront — users know exactly what to prepare before they start.
Numbered steps — "Step 4 of 8" with back-navigation that preserves entered data.
Persistent save & resume — returning users pick up exactly where they left off.
Single-source data — information entered once, auto-filled wherever reused.

What didn't make it into V1

AI-assisted document scanning

Auto-fill of PAN and Aadhaar fields from a camera capture. Deprioritised due to OCR accuracy concerns — moved to roadmap for Q2 next year.

Co-applicant flow

The ability to add a co-applicant mid-flow for joint loan applications. Data showed <4% of users needed this — backlogged post-MVP.

Learnings

What I took away from this

This project pushed me to think beyond UI patterns and into the psychology of financial anxiety. A few things I'd carry into every lending or fintech project going forward:

Trust is a UX problem, not a brand problem

Users didn't distrust the brand — they distrusted the flow. A single sentence explaining why Aadhaar was needed reduced hesitation more than any visual redesign. Context is the feature.

Soft checks protect both sides

The eligibility pre-check wasn't just a UX win — it reduced incomplete applications in the backend by 31%, which was a direct ops cost saving the team hadn't anticipated.

Returning users are your highest-intent segment

The save & resume feature had outsized impact because returning users were already motivated — they just needed the friction removed. Never write off abandonment as disinterest.

Shipping fast doesn't mean skipping research

The 3-week research phase felt like a lot in a startup context. But the 4 features we deprioritised based on data saved at least 6 weeks of engineering time that would've been wasted on low-impact work.
